## What was discovered or achieved?

### Takeaway
Google achieved the first in-orbit test of its Trillium TPU AI chips via a Planet-built satellite on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare, validating whether commercial AI silicon can survive and operate in the radiation, temperature, and vacuum environment of low Earth orbit.

### Cited Findings
- The prototype satellite (internally called "MVP" — Minimum Viable Product) carries four Trillium TPUs powered by approximately 1 kilowatt of solar energy — [Google Project Suncatcher: 4 TPUs Launch to Orbit Oct 1 via tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/google-project-suncatcher-orbital-ai-data-center-2026/) (site blocked, confirmed via search snippet)
- Primary test objectives: validate AI chips against space radiation, launch stress, extreme temperature cycling, and cooling limits — [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/google-to-send-ai-chips-into-orbit-on-oct-1-project-suncatcher)
- A two-satellite launch is planned for 2027 to test optical inter-satellite links between satellites in the same cluster — [Data Center Dynamics search snippet]

### Inferences
- The 1 kW solar power constraint (versus megawatts consumed by ground data centers) means the space-based compute paradigm is currently suited for inference workloads, not large-scale training
- Success of this MVP test is a prerequisite for the 2027 two-satellite optical link test and the eventual 81-satellite cluster design

### Gaps
- No confirmation from Google's official blog or press release was accessible (NASA.gov and Google.com were not checked)
- It is unclear whether the four Trillium chips in the prototype are first-generation or a space-hardened variant

## Secondary Story: SpaceX Crew-13 Sets ISS Docking Speed Record (October 2 SGT)

### Takeaway
SpaceX Crew-13 docked with the ISS at 7:00 PM EDT October 1 (= 7:00 AM SGT October 2), setting the record for the fastest US spacecraft journey to the ISS at 7 hours 50 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Crew-13 launched from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida on October 1, 2026 at 11:10 AM EDT — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-spacex-crew-13-launches-to-international-space-station/)
- Crew: NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA astronaut Joshua Kutryk, Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov — [Space.com live coverage](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-nasa-crew-13-astronauts-launch-to-iss-october-1-2026) (site blocked)
- Docking occurred at 7:00 PM EDT (23:00 UTC = 07:00 SGT October 2) at the Harmony module's forward-facing port — [spacepolicyonline.com](https://spacepolicyonline.com/events/launch-of-crew-13-to-the-iss-sept-12-2026-ccsfs-630-pm-et/)
- Total transit time: 7 hours 50 minutes — described as "the fastest in the history of the International Space Station" for a US spacecraft — [NASA blog](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2026/10/01/nasas-spacex-crew-13-reaches-orbit-news-conference-at-1245-p-m-edt/)
- Hatch opening to ISS occurred at approximately 8:45 PM EDT (08:45 SGT October 2) — [spacepolicyonline.com](https://spacepolicyonline.com/events/launch-of-crew-13-to-the-iss-sept-12-2026-ccsfs-630-pm-et/)
- Crew-12 was preparing for departure to Earth no earlier than October 5 — [NASA news release](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-spacex-crew-13-launches-to-international-space-station/)

### Inferences
- The sub-8-hour ISS docking has been achieved through phasing optimization (minimal orbital looping), suggesting SpaceX and NASA have refined the rapid-rendezvous profile first demonstrated with Russian Soyuz vehicles
